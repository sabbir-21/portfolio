Reactivity Properties and Their Meanings

Ionization Energy ($I$): The energy required to remove an electron from the molecule. The lower the value, the easier the molecule gives up an electron (nucleophilicity).

Electron Affinity ($A$): The tendency of the molecule to accept an electron. The higher the value, the easier the molecule accepts an electron (electrophilicity).

Chemical Potential ($\mu$): A measure of the propensity for electron transfer. Its negativity indicates a tendency to gain electrons.

Electronegativity ($\chi$): A measure of the power of an atom/molecule to attract electrons to itself. It is equal to the negative of the chemical potential ($\chi = -\mu$).

Chemical Hardness ($\eta$): A measure of the molecule's resistance to a change in the number of electrons. High hardness means low reactivity (high stability).

Chemical Softness ($S$): The inverse of hardness ($S = 1/\eta$). High softness means high reactivity.

Electrophilicity Index ($\omega_1$): Measures the power and stability of a molecule to accept electrons (electrophilic power). High $\omega_1$ indicates the molecule is a strong electrophile.

Formulas Used

Koopmans' Approximation:
Ionization Energy ($I$): $$I \approx -E_{\text{HOMO}}$$ Electron Affinity ($A$): $$A \approx -E_{\text{LUMO}}$$

Chemical Potential ($\mu$) / Electronegativity ($\chi$):
$$\mu = -\chi = \frac{E_{\text{HOMO}} + E_{\text{LUMO}}}{2}$$

Chemical Hardness ($\eta$):
$$\eta = \frac{I - A}{2} = \frac{E_{\text{LUMO}} - E_{\text{HOMO}}}{2}$$

Chemical Softness ($S$):
$$S = \frac{1}{\eta}$$

Electrophilicity Index ($\omega_1$):
$$\omega_1 = \frac{\mu^2}{2\eta}$$
